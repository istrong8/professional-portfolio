# Thermal Hydraulics, Computational Fluid Dynamics & Multiphysics

## Overview

This directory documents independent computational engineering projects involving heat transfer, fluid mechanics, computational fluid dynamics (CFD), reactor thermal hydraulics, and coupled multiphysics simulation.

The objective is to investigate thermal and fluid behavior in engineering systems using numerical modeling, analytical comparisons, and reproducible computational methods.

Projects will include applications relevant to nuclear reactors, energy systems, thermal management, and conceptual space fission power systems.

## Research Interests

- Reactor thermal hydraulics
- Steady-state and transient heat transfer
- Conduction, convection, and thermal radiation
- Computational fluid dynamics
- Fluid flow and pressure losses
- Turbulence modeling
- Thermal management and heat rejection
- Coupled neutronics and thermal analysis
- Multiphysics simulation
- Numerical stability and convergence
- Thermal behavior of conceptual space reactor systems

## Computational Tools

Projects may use:

- **MOOSE:** Finite-element thermal and multiphysics simulation
- **OpenFOAM:** Computational fluid dynamics
- **Nek5000 / nekRS:** High-fidelity fluid flow and heat transfer
- **Cardinal:** Coupled Monte Carlo neutronics and multiphysics applications
- **OpenMC:** Neutronics and nuclear heating calculations
- **Gmsh:** Geometry and mesh generation
- **ParaView:** Scientific visualization
- **Python:** Numerical calculations, analysis, and plotting

Software installation and operational status will be documented separately for each project.

## Project Documentation Standards

Each completed project will include:

- Engineering objective and theoretical background
- Governing equations
- Geometry and material properties
- Initial and boundary conditions
- Meshing methods and numerical settings
- Simulation input files and original code
- Results and engineering visualizations
- Mesh and time-step convergence studies
- Analytical verification or applicable benchmark comparisons
- Sensitivity and uncertainty analysis
- Model limitations
- LaTeX technical reports and compiled PDFs
- Reproducibility instructions and references

## Verification and Validation

Numerical simulations will be assessed through appropriate analytical solutions, benchmark comparisons, mesh-refinement studies, conservation checks, and experimental reference data where available.

The successful execution of a simulation does not independently establish physical validation.

## Reproducibility

Projects will identify software versions, input parameters, mesh specifications, numerical solvers, convergence criteria, and relevant computational dependencies.

Large binary output files and restricted datasets will not be included in the public repository.

## Publication Policy

Only independently developed computational models, original documentation, and publicly distributable scientific information will be published.

Employer-owned, proprietary, export-controlled, restricted-licensed, and non-public materials will be excluded without appropriate authorization.
