§ 01

Reactor types

A space reactor is described by how it turns heat into electricity or thrust, what it is for, how heat leaves the core, and what it burns.

Power conversion · static

Static · no moving parts

Thermoelectric

A temperature difference across semiconductor junctions drives a current. Simple and rugged, but only a few percent efficient.

Flown: SNAP-10A, Soviet BES-5 (Buk). Ground: Romashka.

Static · no moving parts

Thermionic

A hot emitter boils off electrons that cross a gap to a cooler collector. Runs very hot, so it suits compact cores and high radiator temperatures.

Flown: TOPAZ (Kosmos 1818, 1867). Ground: TOPAZ-II.

Power conversion · dynamic

Dynamic · reciprocating

Stirling

A sealed working gas is heated and cooled to drive a piston and linear alternator. High efficiency at small sizes.

Tested with fission heat: DUFF (2012), KRUSTY (2017–18).

Dynamic · turbine

Brayton

A closed loop of gas, often helium–xenon, drives a turbine and compressor. Scales well to tens of kilowatts and up.

Planned: SR-1 Freedom (about 20 kWe, closed Brayton). Studied: Prometheus/JIMO.

Dynamic · turbine

Rankine

A working fluid boils and condenses, as in a terrestrial steam plant. Space designs used liquid metal, such as mercury, instead of water.

Ground: SNAP-2 and SNAP-8 used mercury Rankine conversion.

Missions

Propulsion

Nuclear thermal propulsion

The core heats hydrogen directly and expels it through a nozzle. High thrust with about twice the efficiency of chemical engines.

Rover/NERVA, RD-0410, DRACO (cancelled 2025).

Propulsion

Nuclear electric propulsion

The reactor makes electricity for ion or Hall thrusters. Low thrust, very high efficiency, and long operation.

SR-1 Freedom, Prometheus/JIMO (cancelled 2005).

Power

Surface power

A reactor on the Moon or Mars powers a base through the lunar night, polar darkness, and dust storms.

Fission Surface Power, Lunar Reactor-1, Kilopower.

Cooling

Pumped liquid metal

An electromagnetic pump circulates NaK or lithium through the core. Good heat transfer at low pressure, but the pump and loop are single points of failure.

NaK: SNAP-10A, BES-5, TOPAZ. Lithium: SP-100.

Heat pipe

Sealed pipes move heat passively by evaporation and condensation. No pumps, and losing one pipe does not lose the core.

DUFF, KRUSTY/Kilopower.

Gas-cooled Brayton

The Brayton working gas itself cools the core and then drives the turbine. Simple loop and high temperature, at the cost of lower heat-transfer coefficients.

Selected by Naval Reactors in 2005 for Prometheus/JIMO (helium–xenon).

Fuels

U-ZrH

Uranium in zirconium hydride, which moderates neutrons inside the fuel itself. Allows small thermal-spectrum cores.

SNAP-2, SNAP-8, SNAP-10A.

UN

Uranium nitride: dense, with high thermal conductivity, suited to hot fast-spectrum cores.

SP-100.

UO2

Uranium dioxide, the fuel with the deepest terrestrial experience base.

TOPAZ. Planned: SR-1 Freedom (HALEU).

U-Mo

A uranium–molybdenum metal alloy that can be cast as a solid core.

KRUSTY (highly enriched U-Mo).

Enrichment

HEU vs HALEU

HEU · ≥ 20% U-235Smallest, lightest cores. Every reactor flown to date used HEU. It carries heavier security requirements and, under NSPM-20, the highest launch-authorization tier.
HALEU · 5–20% U-235Below the weapons-usable threshold, so security and launch approval are simpler, at the cost of a larger core. Recent US programs (SR-1 Freedom, LR-1) specify HALEU.
Note

Radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs), like those on Voyager, Curiosity, and Perseverance, are radioisotope power systems, not reactors. They make heat from the natural decay of plutonium-238. There is no chain reaction, no criticality, and no control system.
